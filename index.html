Glow – Monitor Brightness

Your Samsung smart monitor's brightness, in your Mac's menu bar.

Samsung Smart Monitors don't support DDC/CI, so a Mac's brightness keys can't change them. Glow talks to the monitor over your home network instead, the way Samsung's own remote does, and changes its real backlight.

What you need

  • A Samsung Smart Monitor from 2022 or later (M5, M7, M8, M9; model codes ending in B, C or D), or a recent Samsung smart TV with IP Remote.
  • macOS 14 Sonoma or later.
  • Your Mac and the monitor on the same network.

Setting up

  1. On the monitor, turn on Settings → Connection → Network → Expert Settings → IP Remote.
  2. Open Glow and click the sun in the menu bar. When macOS asks, allow Glow to find devices on your local network.
  3. Choose your monitor from the list.
  4. Choose Allow on the monitor's screen. That's it: Glow remembers the monitor.

Troubleshooting

My monitor isn't in the list

Check that it's switched on and on the same Wi‑Fi or network as your Mac. Some routers keep 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz apart; put both devices on the same one. You can also type the monitor's IP address, found in its network settings.

Glow says it can see the monitor but can't control it

IP Remote is off. Turn it on (step 1 above), then click Try Again.

The brightness doesn't change

Some picture modes, such as Dynamic or energy saving, lock the brightness. Switch the monitor to the Standard or Movie picture mode.

The monitor no longer recognises my Mac

This can happen after a monitor reset. Click Connect in Glow and choose Allow on the monitor again.
